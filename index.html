What this app does

It ranks all 6,837 DART bus stops by how exposed waiting riders are to summer heat. Planners can then test where a new shade tree or bus shelter would help most.

Heat Exposure Index (0–100)

HEI = 100 × (0.4·pct(LST) + 0.3·(1 − pct(tree index)) + 0.3·SVI)

  • LST: Landsat 8/9 surface temperature, median for June–August 2026.
  • Tree index: Dynamic World V1 tree probability (10 m), June–August 2026. Averaged over a 30 m waiting area around each stop.
  • SVI: CDC/ATSDR Social Vulnerability Index 2022, census tract percentile.
  • Stops: DART GTFS feed, 2026.

Stops, tree cover and surface temperature all come from 2026, so the comparison is between data from the same year. SVI comes from the latest release, which is based on the census.

What-if assumptions

  • Tree: raises the tree index of stops within 30 m in proportion to how much of the waiting area the crown covers. The effect fades with distance.
  • Shelter: gives full overhead shade to stops within 15 m, so their shade term becomes 0.
  • Only shade is modeled. Any cooling effect on surface temperature isn't counted, so the estimated benefit is conservative.

Limitations

  • The index ranks relative exposure. It doesn't predict heat illness.
  • Landsat passes over at about 10:30–11:00 a.m., so it misses the afternoon peak.
  • GTFS data doesn't record existing shelters.
